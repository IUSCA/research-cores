# Budgeting core services in proposals

Verified 2026-10-04 against IU policy, NIH and NSF instructions, and IU core
websites. Sources are listed at the end.

This skill covers only the core-specific parts of a proposal. The companion
`research-funding` repository covers drafting the rest. Route budget and
compliance questions for the whole proposal to the unit's grant
administrator; see `routing-and-submitting-at-iu` there.

## Start early

**Recommended.** Contact each core four or more weeks before the routing
deadline. A core needs time to scope the work, check capacity, and write a
letter.

Bring the core:

- The aims that need the core, and the samples or instrument hours per year.
- The proposal's start date and number of years.
- The sponsor and the mechanism.
- Whether any work will run at another institution.

## Get a quote

Ask for a written quote. A good quote names:

- Each service or instrument, its unit (per sample, per hour, per run), and
  the rate.
- The rate tier. IU cores publish tiers for IU, other academic, and
  commercial users. See `using-iu-research-cores`.
- Extra fees: consultation, training, setup, failed samples, data storage or
  retrieval, and shipping. CMG charges for raw data retrieval if a user loses
  their copy (CMG Data Storage policy, **Observed 2026-10-04**).
- The date the rates take effect.

iLab cannot generate an estimate for someone not yet registered (IUSM iLab
page, **Observed 2026-10-04**). Register first, or ask the core for a quote
outside iLab. Some cores post calculators. CGB offers "an interactive
worksheet to create a non-binding estimate" (CGB page, **Observed
2026-10-04**).

## Rates change; plan for it

**Required.** IU recharge centers submit proposed rates "on at least a
biennial basis." Rates must recover no more than allowable cost, and any
surplus "will be carried forward at year-end to reduce recharge rates"
(FIN-ACC-400).

**Required.** Federal rules let a recipient charge specialized service
facility costs directly "based on actual usage." Rates must not discriminate
between federal and other users, must recover only aggregate costs, and
"must be adjusted at least biennially" (2 CFR 200.468).

**Recommended.** Ask the core what increase to assume for later years. Use
the unit's standard escalation if the core has no guidance. Say in the
justification which you used.

## Where core charges go in the budget

**External.** On the NIH R&R Budget, Other Direct Costs include an
"Equipment or Facility Rental/User Fees" line. The instructions say to
"identify and justify each rental user fee" in the budget justification
(NIH SF424 Forms-I, G.300).

**External.** On an NSF budget, costs not on another line go on line G.6,
Other, and "must be itemized and detailed in the budget justification."
Computer services go on line G.4 "only where it is institutional policy to
charge such costs as direct charges" (NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter II).

**Required.** Core charges are services, so they sit in the MTDC base and
carry F&A. IU's base includes "materials, supplies, services, travel" (IU
Research rates page). The `budgeting-a-proposal` skill in `research-funding`
covers F&A.

**Open item.** No IU guidance found on 2026-10-04 says whether core
recharges belong on the NIH user fees line or on an "Other" line. Ask your
grant administrator, and record IU's answer here.

## Justify the charges

**Recommended.** Write one line per core service:

```text
<Core name> (<campus>), <service>: <units per year> x $<rate> per <unit>
= $<total>. Rates are the core's <tier> rates effective <date>,
<escalation> per year thereafter. Source: core quote dated <date>.
```

Say why the core is used instead of buying equipment. Name the expertise the
core adds, such as experimental design or analysis.

## Letters from cores

**External.** NIH: letters "ensuring access to core facilities and resources
should stipulate whether access will be provided as a fee-for-service."
Letters go in a single Letters of Support PDF (NIH SF424 Forms-I, G.400).

**External.** NSF: send letters of collaboration, not letters of support.
"Letters of collaboration should be limited to stating the intent to
collaborate and should not contain endorsements or evaluation of the
proposed project." Unrequested letters of support may cause the proposal to
be "returned without review" (NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter II). PAPPG offers this
format: "If the proposal submitted by Dr. [insert the full name of the
Principal Investigator] entitled [insert the proposal title] is selected for
funding by NSF, it is my intent to collaborate and/or commit resources as
detailed in the Project Description or the Facilities, Equipment and Other
Resources section of the proposal."

Several IU cores offer letters. The Center for Proteome Analysis lists help
with "grant support letters." CGB assists "with experimental design and grant
proposal development by IU faculty" (their pages, **Observed 2026-10-04**).

**Recommended.** Send the core a draft letter with the services, volumes,
and years filled in. State whether the core is a fee-for-service provider or
a collaborator. Core staff who contribute intellectually may expect
authorship; see `acknowledging-research-cores`.

## Facilities and Other Resources

**External.** NIH requires a Facilities & Other Resources attachment
"unless otherwise specified in the NOFO." It describes how the environment
"contributes to the probability of success." URLs are not allowed unless the
NOFO allows them (NIH SF424 Forms-I, G.220). NSF asks for a Facilities,
Equipment and Other Resources section (NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter II).

**Recommended.** For each core the project uses, write two or three
sentences: its name, campus, the instruments the project needs, and the
expertise it adds. Ask the core for its own description. Do not paste a
core's full instrument list.

UITS Research Technologies publishes a facilities statement for IU research
computing and storage. The Research Data Commons FAQ links it (RDC FAQ,
**Observed 2026-10-04**). The `research-technologies` companion repository
covers those systems.

## Paying for a new shared instrument

When several funded labs need an instrument IU lacks, the NIH S10 programs
may pay for it. **External**, from the NIH ORIP S10 Fact Sheet 2025:

| Program | Award range | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Instrumentation Grant (BIG), limited competition | $25,000 to $350,000 | Basic cell sorters, confocal microscopes, ultramicrotomes |
| Shared Instrumentation Grant (SIG) | $50,000 to $750,000 | Confocal microscopes, MRI, mass spectrometers, NMR, flow cytometers, sequencers |
| High-End Instrumentation Grant (HEI) | $750,001 to $2,000,000 | MRI and PET imagers, cryo-electron microscopes, NMR spectrometers |

The programs require "at least three principal investigators, each with
active NIH-funded research awards," and institutional commitment to support
the instrument. ORIP issues the notices "with one receipt date in June each
year" (ORIP S10 Fact Sheet 2025).

**Recommended.** Plan an S10 with the core that would run the instrument.
The core supplies the usage history and the support plan. Publications that
acknowledge earlier S10 awards strengthen the case; see
`acknowledging-research-cores`.

**Open item.** S10 programs are limited competitions at some institutions.
Ask IU Research whether IU runs an internal selection for S10 applications.

## Keep this file current

- Re-read FIN-ACC-400 and 2 CFR 200.468 each review.
- Check the NIH forms version each review. NIH changes forms by letter, such
  as Forms-I. Update the G.220, G.300, and G.400 links when a new version
  takes effect.
- Check the current PAPPG and its supplements each review.
- Check the ORIP S10 page for new award ranges or receipt dates.
